\title{\bf Minimum Aged Labelling with Capacity Constraints:\\[2pt]
A $\theta$-Threshold Dual-Track Flow-Splitting Routing Algorithm }
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study the \emph{Minimum Aged Labelling with Capacity constraints} (MAL-C) problem: on a directed
graph with edge travel times, assign a set of departure-time labels to every edge and route a set of
freight demands so as to minimize the total number of unit-capacity trucks dispatched, where the flow
on each (edge, time) pair must be rounded \emph{up} to a whole truck. We present a dual-track algorithm
that routes bulky integer flow along shortest paths and consolidates fractional flow onto a Directed
Steiner Forest (DSF) backbone whose departure times are produced by a global \emph{dual-label}
construction. Under a store-and-forward model (freight may wait at nodes at no cost), we prove that the
labels make every source--destination pair \emph{temporally connected}---the canonical route is a
temporal path, whether the endpoints lie in one strongly connected component or across several---and
that every backbone edge carries at most two distinct departure labels, so that fractional flows from
different demands genuinely share trucks. Balancing the two tracks through a splitting threshold
$\theta$ yields an approximation ratio of $O\!\left(\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3} + n^{2/3}\right)$, where $n=|V|$
and $h$ is the number of demands.
\end{abstract}

%====================================================================
\section{Problem Description}
%====================================================================

We study the \emph{Minimum Aged Labelling with Capacity constraints} (MAL-C) problem, a temporal
network-design problem that augments Minimum Aged Labelling with truck-capacity constraints. 

\paragraph{Input.}
\begin{itemize}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
  \item A directed graph $G=(V,E)$ with an edge travel-time function $w:E\to\mathbb{N}$; we write
        $n=|V|$. Freight that enters $e$ at time $t$ arrives at the head of $e$ at time $t+w(e)$.
  \item A set of $h$ demands $D=\{d_1,\dots,d_h\}$. Each demand $d_i$ specifies a source
        $\src(d_i)$, a destination $\dst(d_i)$, and a freight amount $\dem(d_i)\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Output.}
For every edge $e\in E$ a set of departure times---the \emph{label set}
$\lambda(e)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$---together with, for every demand, a routing of its freight
from source to destination. We write $f_i(e,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ for the flow of demand $i$ that enters edge $e$
at time $t$ (this flow reaches the head of $e$ at time $t+w(e)$). A feasible output satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt,label=(\roman*)]
  \item \emph{Flow conservation:} for each demand $d_i$ the routing carries $\dem(d_i)$ units from
        $\src(d_i)$ to $\dst(d_i)$, respecting travel times.
  \item \emph{Label--flow consistency:} for all $e\in E$, $t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ and
        $i\in\{1,\dots,h\}$, if $f_i(e,t)>0$ then $t\in\lambda(e)$; that is, flow may leave an edge
        only at one of its labels.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Objective.}
Trucks have capacity normalized to $1$, and even a fractional total flow on an $(e,t)$ pair forces a
whole truck (\emph{unconditional round-up}). The goal is to minimize the total number of trucks
\begin{equation}\label{eq:objective}
  \min \;\sum_{e\in E}\;\sum_{t}\;
  \left\lceil \sum_{i=1}^{h} f_i(e,t) \right\rceil .
\end{equation}

%====================================================================
\section{Algorithm Design}
%====================================================================

Our algorithm couples two routing tracks through a splitting threshold $\theta\in(0,1)$. Bulky integer
flow travels directly along shortest paths (SP) of $G$, whereas sparse fractional flow---which would
otherwise force each demand to dispatch its own near-empty truck---is consolidated onto a shared
backbone. We describe the ingredients as subroutines before stating the pseudocode.

\paragraph{Backbone via Directed Steiner Forest.}
Given a directed graph with edge costs and a set of ordered terminal pairs, the \emph{Directed Steiner
Forest} (DSF) problem asks for a minimum-cost subgraph that contains a directed path from $s$ to $t$
for every prescribed pair $(s,t)$. Running the polynomial-time $O(n^{2/3+\epsilon})$-approximation of
Berman, Bhattacharyya, Makarychev, Raskhodnikova and Yaroslavtsev~\cite{BBMRY13} (for any fixed
$\epsilon>0$) on the terminal pairs $\{(\src(d_i),\dst(d_i))\}_{i=1}^{h}$ with unit edge costs yields a
backbone $\ESF\subseteq E$ that connects every source to its destination. We write
$\textsc{Build-Backbone}(G,D)$ for this step, returning $\ESF$.

\paragraph{Shortest-path routing with induced labels.}
$\textsc{Route-SP}(s,t,g)$ sends $g$ units along a shortest $s$--$t$ path
$P=(s=v_0,v_1,\dots,v_\ell=t)$ and labels its edges so that they form a temporal path. All freight
departs its source at time $0$ (consistent with our omission of release times), so the label of the
$k$-th edge $(v_{k-1},v_k)$ is simply the time at which the freight reaches its tail $v_{k-1}$, i.e.\
the cumulative travel time
\[
  \ell_k \;=\; \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} w(v_{j-1},v_j),
\]
which is added to $\lambda(v_{k-1}v_k)$. For instance, on $a\to b\to c$ the freight leaves $a$ at time
$0$, so $(a,b)$ receives label $0$; if $w(a,b)=3$ it reaches $b$ at time $3$, so $(b,c)$ receives label
$3$ (in general the next edge is labelled with the arrival time $t_j+w(e_j)$ at its tail); and so on
down the path. Every edge therefore departs exactly when the flow arrives at it, so the flow rides a
single temporal path and reaches $t$ after $\sum_{e\in P}w(e)$ time. We write $\len(\SP_{s,t})$ for the
number of edges on the shortest path.

\paragraph{The dual-track split.}
We split $\dem(d_i)=d_I+d_f$ into its integer part $d_I=\lfloor\dem(d_i)\rfloor$ and fractional part
$d_f\in[0,1)$. The integer part is always sent by $\textsc{Route-SP}$. The fractional part is governed
by $\theta$: if $d_f\le 2\theta$ the entire $d_f$ is placed on the backbone; otherwise a piece of size
exactly $\theta$ is placed on the backbone and the remaining $d_f-\theta$ is sent by
$\textsc{Route-SP}$. Every unit placed on the backbone is pushed along its \emph{canonical route}
(Definition~\ref{def:canonical})---the temporal path built from the dual labels of
Section~\ref{sec:feasibility}---by the subroutine $\textsc{Route-SF}(s,t,g)$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{$\theta$-Threshold Dual-Track Flow Splitting}
\label{alg:dualtrack}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Graph $G=(V,E)$ with travel times $w$, demand set $D$ ($|D|=h$), threshold $\theta\in(0,1)$.
\State $\ESF \gets \textsc{Build-Backbone}(G,D)$
       \Comment{DSF approximation on the terminal pairs}
\State $\lambda_{\mathrm{SF}} \gets \textsc{Assign-Dual-Labels}(\ESF,w)$
       \Comment{Algorithm~\ref{alg:duallabels}}
\For{each demand $d_i\in D$, with $s=\src(d_i)$ and $t=\dst(d_i)$}
  \State $d_I\gets\lfloor\dem(d_i)\rfloor$;\quad $d_f\gets\dem(d_i)-d_I$
  \State $\textsc{Route-SP}(s,t,d_I)$ \Comment{integer flow on a shortest path}
  \If{$d_f \le 2\theta$}
     \State $\textsc{Route-SF}(s,t,d_f)$ \Comment{all fractional flow on the backbone}
  \Else
     \State $\textsc{Route-SF}(s,t,\theta)$;\quad $\textsc{Route-SP}(s,t,\,d_f-\theta)$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return the labelling $\lambda$ and the routing $\{f_i\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

It remains to specify $\textsc{Assign-Dual-Labels}$ and the canonical route it induces; this is the
subject of Section~\ref{sec:feasibility}, where we also prove that the labelled backbone is temporally
connected and that each of its edges carries at most two labels.

%====================================================================
\section{Temporal Feasibility and the Two-Label Guarantee}\label{sec:feasibility}
%====================================================================

For the backbone to let different demands share trucks, its labels must (i) make every terminal pair
\emph{temporally connected}, so that all fractional flow can actually be delivered, and (ii) place few
labels on each edge, so that consolidation is not undone by mismatched departure times. We prove both
properties for \textsc{Assign-Dual-Labels}. Throughout we use one modelling assumption.

\begin{assumption}[Free store-and-forward]\label{ass:wait}
Freight may wait at any node for an arbitrary time at no cost. This is consistent with the
objective~\eqref{eq:objective}, which charges only for trucks dispatched on edges and never for
freight resting at a node.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Temporal graphs and the label construction}

We use the following standard temporal-network vocabulary, specialised to edge-weighted travel times.

\begin{definition}[Temporal path]\label{def:temporal}
Let $(G,\lambda)$ be a temporal graph: a digraph $G=(V,E)$ together with a label function
$\lambda:E\to 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}}$, where $\lambda(e)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}$ is
the set of available times (labels) of edge $e$. A \emph{temporal path} from $u$ to $v$ is a sequence
\[
  (e_1,t_1),(e_2,t_2),\dots,(e_\ell,t_\ell)
\]
subject to the two conditions
\begin{itemize}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Path connectivity:} each $e_i\in E$, and $e_1e_2\cdots e_\ell$ is a walk from $u$ to $v$
        in $G$;
  \item \emph{Time monotonicity:} $t_i\in\lambda(e_i)$ for every $i$, and, writing $e_i=(v_i,v_{i+1})$,
        \[
          t_{i+1}\;\ge\;t_i+w(e_i)\qquad(1\le i<\ell).
        \]
\end{itemize}
The monotonicity condition encodes travel time: departing $v_i$ at $t_i$, the freight reaches
$v_{i+1}$ at time $t_i+w(e_i)$, so the next departure $t_{i+1}$ must be no earlier than that arrival.
(In the classical unit-time case $w\equiv 1$ it reduces to the strict increase
$t_1<t_2<\dots<t_\ell$.) We call $(G,\lambda)$ \emph{temporally connected} on a set of vertex pairs if
every such pair admits a temporal path.
\end{definition}

We now describe \textsc{Assign-Dual-Labels} (Algorithm~\ref{alg:duallabels}) in four stages.

\paragraph{Decomposition.} Contract every strongly connected component (SCC) of $\ESF$ to a single
node; the result is a directed acyclic graph. We list its components in a \emph{topological order}
$C_1,\dots,C_K$---an ordering in which every edge between two distinct components runs from an earlier
to a later one. An edge of $\ESF$ whose endpoints lie in \emph{different} components is a \emph{cross
edge}; every other edge lies inside a single component.

\paragraph{Rooted trees.} Fix a component $C_i$ and let $\ESF[C_i]$ be the subgraph of $\ESF$ induced
on the vertices of $C_i$; being an SCC of $\ESF$, it is strongly connected. We pick a center
$h_i\in C_i$ and define the weighted shortest-path distances \emph{inside $\ESF[C_i]$}: $\din(x)$ from
$x$ to $h_i$, and $\dout(x)$ from $h_i$ to $x$. Because $\ESF[C_i]$ is strongly connected, every
vertex both reaches and is reached from $h_i$ using only edges of $\ESF$, so these distances are
finite and admit shortest-path arborescences that \emph{span} $C_i$ and consist solely of $\ESF$
edges: the reverse \emph{in-tree} $\Tin$ (edges oriented towards $h_i$, one shortest $x$--$h_i$ path
per vertex) and the forward \emph{out-tree} $\Tout$ (edges oriented away from $h_i$). By optimal
substructure, every in-tree edge $(x,x')$ satisfies $\din(x)=w(x,x')+\din(x')$ and every out-tree edge
$(y,y')$ satisfies $\dout(y')=\dout(y)+w(y,y')$; these are exactly the recurrences invoked in
Lemma~\ref{lem:intra}, and since both trees lie in $\ESF$ every canonical route uses only labelled
edges. Let $\Din=\max_x\din(x)$ and $\Dout=\max_x\dout(x)$ be the in- and out-radii of $C_i$, and set
the cross-edge label $t_{\mathrm{cross}}(x,y)=T_i+\Din+\Dout$ for a cross edge leaving $x\in C_i$.

\paragraph{Base times.} Each component receives a \emph{base time} $T_i$, computed by a dynamic
program over the topological order. We set $T_1=0$ and, for $i\ge 2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:basetime}
  T_i \;=\; \max\Big\{\,T_{i-1}+1,\;\;
      \max_{\substack{(x,y)\in\ESF\\ x\in C_j,\ y\in C_i,\ j<i}}
      \big(t_{\mathrm{cross}}(x,y)+w(x,y)\big)\Big\}.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side depends only on earlier components, so the recurrence is well defined. Intuitively
$T_i$ is chosen large enough to (a) start $C_i$ after every earlier component's schedule and (b)
dominate the arrival time of every cross edge that enters $C_i$; property (b) is used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cross}.

\paragraph{Labels.} Finally each edge $e=(x,y)$, with $x\in C_i$, is labelled according to its role:
\begin{itemize}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=3pt]
  \item if $e\in\Tin$ (an in-tree edge):\quad $t_{\mathrm{in}}(e)=T_i+\Din-\din(x)$;
  \item if $e\in\Tout$ (an out-tree edge):\quad $t_{\mathrm{out}}(e)=T_i+\Din+\dout(x)$;
  \item if $e$ is a cross edge:\quad $t_{\mathrm{cross}}(e)=T_i+\Din+\Dout$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Global Dual-Label Construction}
\label{alg:duallabels}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{Assign-Dual-Labels}{$\ESF,w$}
  \State contract the SCCs of $\ESF$ into a DAG; list the components $C_1,\dots,C_K$ in topological order.
  \For{each component $C_i$}
     \State pick a center $h_i$; build, within $\ESF[C_i]$, the in-tree $\Tin$ (depths $\din$) and out-tree $\Tout$ (depths $\dout$);
     \State $\Din\gets\max_x\din(x)$;\quad $\Dout\gets\max_x\dout(x)$.
  \EndFor
  \For{$i=1,\dots,K$}
     \State set the base time $T_i$ by~\eqref{eq:basetime}.
  \EndFor
  \State $\lambda(e)\gets\emptyset$ for all $e\in\ESF$.
  \For{each edge $e=(x,y)\in\ESF$, with $x\in C_i$}
     \State add $t_{\mathrm{in}}(e)=T_i+\Din-\din(x)$ to $\lambda(e)$ if $e\in\Tin$;
     \State add $t_{\mathrm{out}}(e)=T_i+\Din+\dout(x)$ to $\lambda(e)$ if $e\in\Tout$;
     \State add $t_{\mathrm{cross}}(e)=T_i+\Din+\Dout$ to $\lambda(e)$ if $e$ is a cross edge.
  \EndFor
  \State \Return $\lambda_{\mathrm{SF}}$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Temporal connectivity of the labelled backbone}

The algorithm routes backbone flow in a fixed way, which we name once and for all.

\begin{definition}[Canonical route]\label{def:canonical}
For a terminal pair $(u,v)$ joined through the backbone, the \emph{canonical route} $R(u,v)$ is the
walk produced by \textsc{Route-SF}: inside each component it climbs the in-tree from the entry node to
the center $h_i$ and then descends the out-tree to the exit node, visiting the components in
topological order and joining consecutive ones by a cross edge. When $u,v$ lie in the same component
$C_i$ the route is
\[
  u \to \cdots \to h_i \to \cdots \to v,
\]
the in-tree ascent $u\to\cdots\to h_i$ followed by the out-tree descent $h_i\to\cdots\to v$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Intra-component temporal path]\label{lem:intra}
Let $u,v$ lie in the same component $C_i$, and suppose the freight is available at $u$ by time
$T_i+\Din-\din(u)$. Then the canonical route $u\to\cdots\to h_i\to\cdots\to v$ is a temporal path: the
freight departs each edge exactly at its label, reaches $h_i$ at time $T_i+\Din$, and reaches $v$ at
time $T_i+\Din+\dout(v)$. In particular $u$ and $v$ are temporally connected, and the segment occupies
the time window $[\,T_i,\;T_i+\Din+\Dout\,]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Ascent.} Along the in-tree path $u=x_0\to\cdots\to x_p=h_i$ with $e_k=(x_k,x_{k+1})\in\Tin$ we
have $\din(x_k)=w(e_k)+\din(x_{k+1})$. Departing $x_k$ at $t_{\mathrm{in}}(e_k)=T_i+\Din-\din(x_k)$, the
freight reaches $x_{k+1}$ at
\[
  \big(T_i+\Din-\din(x_k)\big)+w(e_k)=T_i+\Din-\din(x_{k+1})=t_{\mathrm{in}}(e_{k+1}),
\]
so each hop is time-respecting with no waiting, and the freight reaches $h_i$ at $T_i+\Din$ (as
$\din(h_i)=0$).

\emph{Descent.} Along the out-tree path $h_i=y_0\to\cdots\to y_q=v$ with $g_k=(y_k,y_{k+1})\in\Tout$
and $\dout(y_{k+1})=\dout(y_k)+w(g_k)$, the first edge departs $h_i$ at
$t_{\mathrm{out}}(g_0)=T_i+\Din+\dout(h_i)=T_i+\Din$, exactly the arrival at $h_i$; inductively the
freight reaches $y_{k+1}$ at $T_i+\Din+\dout(y_{k+1})=t_{\mathrm{out}}(g_{k+1})$. The concatenation is
a temporal path ending at $T_i+\Din+\dout(v)\le T_i+\Din+\Dout$.
\end{proof}

Recall that a \emph{cross edge} is an edge of $\ESF$ whose endpoints lie in different components; by
the topological order it runs from an earlier component to a later one.

\begin{lemma}[Cross edges preserve temporality]\label{lem:cross}
Let $c=(a,b)$ be a cross edge with $a\in C_i$ and $b\in C_j$ (so $i<j$). Under the base
times~\eqref{eq:basetime}, the freight reaches $a$ no later than $t_{\mathrm{cross}}(c)=T_i+\Din+\Dout$,
departs $c$ at that label, and reaches $b$ by time $T_j$. Hence the cross hop together with the
hand-off into $C_j$ is time-respecting.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the canonical route, $a$ is the exit node of $C_i$, reached by descending the out-tree; hence by
the descent in Lemma~\ref{lem:intra} the freight arrives at $a$ at time
\[
  T_i+\Din+\dout(a)\;\le\;T_i+\Din+\Dout=t_{\mathrm{cross}}(c),
\]
using $\dout(a)\le\Dout$. Since $t_{\mathrm{cross}}(c)$ is the only label of $c$, the freight (waiting
if necessary) departs $c$ at $t_{\mathrm{cross}}(c)$ and arrives at $b$ at $t_{\mathrm{cross}}(c)+w(c)$.
By the choice of $T_j$ in~\eqref{eq:basetime}, $T_j\ge t_{\mathrm{cross}}(c)+w(c)$, so the freight
reaches $b$ by $T_j$. Finally the in-tree departure at $b$ is $T_j+D^{(j)}_{\mathrm{in}}-\din(b)\ge T_j$,
so the freight waits at $b$ and continues up $C_j$'s in-tree; the hand-off is time-respecting.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The labelled backbone is temporally connected]\label{thm:temporal}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:wait}, for every terminal pair $(u,v)$ joined through the backbone---within
one component or across several---the canonical route $R(u,v)$ is a temporal path. In particular
$(\ESF,\lambda_{\mathrm{SF}})$ is temporally connected on all terminal pairs, so all backbone demands
can be routed simultaneously.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induct on the number $m$ of cross edges of $R(u,v)$. If $m=0$ then $u,v$ share a component and $R(u,v)$
is a temporal path by Lemma~\ref{lem:intra}. For the step, assume the prefix of $R(u,v)$ delivers the
freight to the entry node of component $C_{i_l}$ by time $T_{i_l}$; this holds initially, since the
source is released at its in-tree label. The segment inside $C_{i_l}$ is a temporal path by
Lemma~\ref{lem:intra} (the entry arrives by $T_{i_l}$, which is at most its in-tree departure), and the
following cross edge with the hand-off into $C_{i_{l+1}}$ is time-respecting by Lemma~\ref{lem:cross},
delivering the freight to the next entry node by $T_{i_{l+1}}$. Splicing time-respecting sub-walks
yields a temporal path.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two labels per edge and consolidation]\label{thm:twolabels}
\textsc{Assign-Dual-Labels} assigns at most two distinct labels to every backbone edge, and under
Assumption~\ref{ass:wait} all fractional flows routed on a common edge depart on those labels. Hence
each backbone edge incurs at most two round-ups in the objective~\eqref{eq:objective}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Inside a component the in-tree and the out-tree are arborescences of opposite orientation (towards,
respectively away from, the center). A directed edge $e=(x,y)$ may belong to the in-tree---carrying
ascending traffic with label $t_{\mathrm{in}}(e)$---and \emph{simultaneously} to the out-tree---carrying
descending traffic with label $t_{\mathrm{out}}(e)$---as happens on a directed cycle
$h_i\to x\to y\to h_i$, but to no third role; a cross edge lies on neither tree and carries only
$t_{\mathrm{cross}}(e)$. Hence every edge has at most two labels. By Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal} each
demand's canonical route is a temporal path that leaves $e$ exactly on the label of the role in which
it uses $e$, so all demands using $e$ in the same role depart together; the trucks on $e$ therefore
number at most $\lceil f_{t_1}(e)\rceil+\lceil f_{t_2}(e)\rceil$, i.e.\ at most two round-ups.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A worked example: assigning the labels}\label{sec:example}

We now give an example illustrating how \textsc{Assign-Dual-Labels} assigns the labels. Let $C_1$ and
$C_2$ be two strongly connected components (SCCs) of the backbone $\ESF$; for concreteness we take
each to be a small directed triangle and focus only on how the labels arise:
\[
  C_1:\ a\to b\to c\to a,\qquad C_2:\ p\to q\to r\to p,
\]
joined by a single cross edge $c\to q$, with travel times
\[
  w(a,b)=2,\ w(b,c)=1,\ w(c,a)=3;\quad
  w(p,q)=w(q,r)=w(r,p)=1;\quad w(c,q)=2 .
\]
Contracting each triangle to a point leaves the two-vertex DAG $C_1\to C_2$; hence the topological
order is $C_1\prec C_2$ and $c\to q$ is the only cross edge (Figure~\ref{fig:example}).

\paragraph{Component $C_1$ ($T_1=0$).} Take the center $h_1=a$. The shortest-path trees rooted at $a$
inside $C_1$ are the in-tree $b\to c\to a$ (with $\din(a)=0$, $\din(c)=3$, $\din(b)=4$) and the
out-tree $a\to b\to c$ (with $\dout(a)=0$, $\dout(b)=2$, $\dout(c)=3$), so $D^{(1)}_{\mathrm{in}}=4$ and
$D^{(1)}_{\mathrm{out}}=3$. Applying $t_{\mathrm{in}}=T_1+D^{(1)}_{\mathrm{in}}-\din(x)$ and
$t_{\mathrm{out}}=T_1+D^{(1)}_{\mathrm{in}}+\dout(x)$ to each edge:
\[
  \begin{array}{l@{\qquad}l}
    \text{in-tree }(b,c):\ t_{\mathrm{in}}=0+4-4=0,  & \text{in-tree }(c,a):\ t_{\mathrm{in}}=0+4-3=1,\\[2pt]
    \text{out-tree }(a,b):\ t_{\mathrm{out}}=0+4+0=4, & \text{out-tree }(b,c):\ t_{\mathrm{out}}=0+4+2=6.
  \end{array}
\]
Edge $(b,c)$ lies on \emph{both} trees, so it carries the two labels $\{0,6\}$ (illustrating
Theorem~\ref{thm:twolabels}); every other edge carries one.

\emph{An intra-component demand $c\to b$.} Its canonical route climbs the in-tree to the center and
then descends the out-tree, i.e.\ $c\to a\to b$, giving the schedule (edge\,@\,departure time)
\[
  (c,a)\,@\,1\ \longrightarrow\ (a,b)\,@\,4,
\]
which delivers the freight to the center $a$ at time $T_1+D^{(1)}_{\mathrm{in}}=4$ and to $b$ at time
$T_1+D^{(1)}_{\mathrm{in}}+\dout(b)=6$. Each departure is at least the previous arrival, so it is a
temporal path, exactly as Lemma~\ref{lem:intra} predicts.

\paragraph{The cross edge and the base time $T_2$.} Edge $c\to q$ receives the single label
\[
  t_{\mathrm{cross}}(c,q)=T_1+D^{(1)}_{\mathrm{in}}+D^{(1)}_{\mathrm{out}}=0+4+3=7,
\]
the latest timestamp $C_1$ ever issues. The base-time recurrence~\eqref{eq:basetime} then gives
\[
  T_2=\max\{\,T_1+1,\ t_{\mathrm{cross}}(c,q)+w(c,q)\,\}=\max\{\,1,\ 7+2\,\}=9,
\]
so $C_2$ opens late enough to catch whatever arrives through $c\to q$.

\paragraph{Component $C_2$ ($T_2=9$).} With center $h_2=p$, the in-tree $q\to r\to p$ has
$\din(p)=0$, $\din(r)=1$, $\din(q)=2$ and the out-tree $p\to q\to r$ has $\dout(p)=0$, $\dout(q)=1$,
$\dout(r)=2$, so $D^{(2)}_{\mathrm{in}}=D^{(2)}_{\mathrm{out}}=2$ and
\[
  \begin{array}{l@{\qquad}l}
    \text{in-tree }(q,r):\ t_{\mathrm{in}}=9+2-2=9,   & \text{in-tree }(r,p):\ t_{\mathrm{in}}=9+2-1=10,\\[2pt]
    \text{out-tree }(p,q):\ t_{\mathrm{out}}=9+2+0=11, & \text{out-tree }(q,r):\ t_{\mathrm{out}}=9+2+1=12.
  \end{array}
\]
Again $(q,r)$ carries two labels, $\{9,12\}$.

\emph{An inter-component demand $a\to r$.} Its canonical route descends $C_1$'s out-tree to the exit
$c$, crosses to $q$, then climbs $C_2$'s in-tree to $p$ and descends the out-tree to $r$:
\[
  a\to b\to c\ \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{cross}\ }\ q\to r\to p\to q\to r,
\]
with schedule
\[
  (a,b)\,@\,4\ \to\ (b,c)\,@\,6\ \to\ (c,q)\,@\,7\ \to\ (q,r)\,@\,9\ \to\ (r,p)\,@\,10\ \to\ (p,q)\,@\,11\ \to\ (q,r)\,@\,12,
\]
arriving at $r$ at time $13$. Every departure is at least the preceding arrival, so the whole route is
a temporal path (Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal}). The cross label $7$ and the base time $T_2=9$ dovetail:
the freight leaves $C_1$ at its latest time $7$ and reaches $q$ at $7+w(c,q)=9=T_2$, exactly when
$C_2$'s schedule opens.

\section{Approximation-Ratio Analysis}\label{sec:analysis}
%====================================================================

We compare the algorithm's cost $\ALG=\ALGSP+\ALGSF$ against the true optimum $\OPT$ of
\eqref{eq:objective}. Although $\OPT$ is unknown, it is bounded below by two ideal quantities.

\begin{definition}[Lower bounds]\label{def:lb}
\leavevmode
\begin{itemize}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Fractional-flow bound $\OPTfrac$:} the cost when fractional trucks are permitted (no
        round-up) and all flow travels along shortest paths,
        $\OPTfrac=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\dem(d_i)\cdot\len(\SP_i)$,
        where $\len(\SP_i)$ is the number of edges on a shortest $\src(d_i)$--$\dst(d_i)$ path.
        Since every unit of flow is forced to round up in reality, $\OPT\ge\OPTfrac$.
  \item \textbf{Connectivity bound $\OPTSF$:} regardless of how small a flow is, each source must be
        physically connected to its destination, so the optimum solution contains a Directed Steiner
        Forest; with unit edge cost, the minimum DSF edge count is a floor, $\OPT\ge\OPTSF$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Throughout, $\rho_{\mathrm{DSF}}=O(n^{2/3})$ denotes the approximation factor of the DSF
routine~\cite{BBMRY13} invoked in line~1 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:dualtrack} (we suppress the arbitrarily
small $\epsilon>0$), so the backbone satisfies
$|\ESF|\le \rho_{\mathrm{DSF}}\cdot\OPTSF=O(n^{2/3})\cdot\OPTSF$.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Cost of the shortest-path (SP) track}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

The SP track carries (i) the integer parts $d_I$ of every demand and (ii) the leftover fractional
pieces $x=d_f-\theta$ for demands with $d_f>2\theta$.

\paragraph{Integer flow.} Integer flow never wastes a truck: on any edge the rounded value of a sum
of integers equals the sum itself, $\lceil\sum d_I\rceil=\sum d_I$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spint}
  \ALGSP^{\mathrm{int}}=\sum_{i=1}^{h} d_I^{(i)}\cdot\len(\SP_i)
  \;\le\;\sum_{i=1}^{h}\dem(d_i)\cdot\len(\SP_i)=\OPTfrac .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Leftover fractional flow.} By the splitting rule, a fractional piece reaches the SP track
only when $d_f>2\theta$, in which case its size satisfies the key inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xtheta}
  x=d_f-\theta \;>\; 2\theta-\theta=\theta .
\end{equation}
On the SP track each such piece is rounded up to $\lceil x\rceil=1$ truck per edge. Using
$x>\theta\Rightarrow 1<x/\theta$ from \eqref{eq:xtheta},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spfrac}
  \ALGSP^{\mathrm{frac}}
  =\sum_{i:\,d_f>2\theta} 1\cdot\len(\SP_i)
  \;<\;\sum_{i:\,d_f>2\theta}\frac{x_i}{\theta}\,\len(\SP_i)
  =\frac1\theta\sum_{i:\,d_f>2\theta} x_i\,\len(\SP_i)
  \;\le\;\frac1\theta\,\OPTfrac ,
\end{equation}

Adding \eqref{eq:spint} and \eqref{eq:spfrac},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sp}
  \boxed{\;\ALGSP \;\le\; \Big(1+\tfrac1\theta\Big)\OPTfrac
  \;=\; O\!\Big(\tfrac1\theta\Big)\cdot\OPT.\;}
\end{equation}
Intuitively: the smaller $\theta$ is, the lower the threshold for diverting flow to the SP track, and
the larger the round-up blow-up factor $1/\theta$.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Cost of the Steiner-forest (SF) track}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

By the splitting rule, every demand injects at most $2\theta$ of flow into the backbone (it injects
$d_f\le 2\theta$ when $d_f\le 2\theta$, and exactly $\theta$ when $d_f>2\theta$). Let demand $i$ route
its backbone flow $s_i\le 2\theta$ along a path $R_i\subseteq\ESF$, and let $F_e=\sum_{i:\,e\in R_i}s_i$
be the total flow on backbone edge $e$.

By Theorem~\ref{thm:twolabels} each backbone edge carries at most two labels with flows
$f_{t_1}(e),f_{t_2}(e)$ summing to $F_e$. Since $\lceil z\rceil\le z+1$ for $z\ge 0$, the truck count
on a single edge obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peredge}
  \lceil f_{t_1}(e)\rceil+\lceil f_{t_2}(e)\rceil \;\le\; F_e+2 .
\end{equation}
Summing \eqref{eq:peredge} over all backbone edges,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sfsum}
  \ALGSF \;\le\; \sum_{e\in\ESF}\big(F_e+2\big)
  \;=\;\underbrace{\sum_{e\in\ESF}F_e}_{\text{flow term}}
  \;+\;\underbrace{2\,|\ESF|}_{\text{infrastructure term}} .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Flow term.} Each backbone path has at most $|\ESF|$ edges, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flowterm}
  \sum_{e\in\ESF}F_e=\sum_{i=1}^{h} s_i\,|R_i|
  \;\le\; 2\theta\sum_{i=1}^{h}|R_i|
  \;\le\; 2\theta\cdot h\cdot|\ESF|
  \;
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Infrastructure term.} Even an edge that carries only an infinitesimal flow must dispatch a
whole truck per label; with at most two labels this is at most $2$ trucks per edge, giving the
$2|\ESF|$ term. \emph{This term is independent of $\theta$ and must not be dropped:} it is the
unavoidable cost of physically operating the backbone.

Combining \eqref{eq:sfsum}--\eqref{eq:flowterm} and $|\ESF|\le O(n^{2/3})\OPTSF$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sf}
  \boxed{\;\ALGSF \;\le\; 2(h\theta+1)\,|\ESF|
  \;=\; O\!\big((h\theta+1)\,n^{2/3}\big)\cdot\OPTSF
  \;\le\; O\!\big((h\theta+1)\,n^{2/3}\big)\cdot\OPT.\;}
\end{equation}
Intuitively: the larger $\theta$ is, the more total flow you allow into the backbone and the more the
flow term grows; the additive $n^{2/3}$ is the price of building the backbone at all.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Balancing the bounds and the final ratio}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

For a fixed threshold $\theta$, let $\mathrm{TotalRatio}(\theta)$ denote the upper bound our analysis
proves on the approximation ratio $\ALG/\OPT=(\ALGSP+\ALGSF)/\OPT$ achieved by
Algorithm~\ref{alg:dualtrack} with that $\theta$. Adding the SP bound~\eqref{eq:sp} and the SF
bound~\eqref{eq:sf} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total}
  \frac{\ALG}{\OPT}\;\le\;\mathrm{TotalRatio}(\theta)
  \;=\; O\!\Big(\tfrac1\theta\Big)
  \;+\; O\!\big(h\theta\,n^{2/3}\big)
  \;+\; O\!\big(n^{2/3}\big).
\end{equation}
Only the first two terms depend on $\theta$. Minimizing $\tfrac1\theta+h\theta n^{2/3}$ by equating
the two,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thetastar}
  \frac1\theta = h\theta\,n^{2/3}
  \;\Longrightarrow\; \theta^2=\frac{1}{h\,n^{2/3}}
  \;\Longrightarrow\; \theta^\ast=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3}}\in(0,1).
\end{equation}
At $\theta^\ast$ each of the first two terms equals $\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3}$, while the third is unchanged,
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:final}
  \boxed{\;\mathrm{TotalRatio}(\theta^\ast)
  \;=\; O\!\big(\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3}+n^{2/3}\big)
  \;=\; O\!\big(\max\{\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3},\,n^{2/3}\}\big).\;}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Approximation guarantee]\label{thm:main}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:wait}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:dualtrack} with threshold
$\theta^\ast=1/(\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3})$ produces a feasible routing and labelling whose cost is within a
factor $O\!\big(\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3}+n^{2/3}\big)$ of the optimum of~\eqref{eq:objective}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Two regimes]
The bound \eqref{eq:final} separates into two regimes, with the crossover at $h=n^{2/3}$:
\[
  \mathrm{TotalRatio}(\theta^\ast)=
  \begin{cases}
    O\!\big(\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3}\big), & h\ge n^{2/3}\ \ (\text{round-up dominated}),\\[4pt]
    O\!\big(n^{2/3}\big),           & h< n^{2/3}\ \ (\text{backbone dominated}).
  \end{cases}
\]
When demands are plentiful the dual-track splitting is what limits the cost; when demands are few the
cost is governed by the cost of the Directed Steiner Forest itself, i.e.\ by the DSF approximation
factor $\rho_{\mathrm{DSF}}=O(n^{2/3})$. No choice of $\theta$ removes the $n^{2/3}$ term, since the
backbone must be built to enable consolidation.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[On a common pitfall]
A naive analysis drops the infrastructure term $2|\ESF|$ in \eqref{eq:sfsum}, folding it into
$O(h\theta\,n^{2/3})$. That is valid only when $h\theta\ge 1$. At the balancing optimum, however,
$h\theta^\ast=\sqrt{h}/n^{1/3}<1$ whenever $h<n^{2/3}$, so the additive term in fact \emph{dominates}
and cannot be absorbed. Retaining it yields the honest bound \eqref{eq:final}; omitting it would
incorrectly claim $O(\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3})$.
\end{remark}

%====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%====================================================================

We presented a dual-track routing-and-labelling algorithm for the MAL-C problem. The
$\theta$-threshold rule sends bulky integer flow along shortest paths and consolidates fractional
flow onto a Directed Steiner Forest backbone. The global dual-label construction equips the backbone
with a temporally consistent schedule: under a free store-and-forward model the canonical route of
every terminal pair is a temporal path (Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal})---intra-component segments are
exactly synchronized and cross-component hops are absorbed by base times that dominate all incoming
arrivals---so that each backbone edge carries at most two labels (Theorem~\ref{thm:twolabels}) and
fractional flows from different demands genuinely share trucks. A careful, complete cost
analysis---retaining the $\theta$-independent backbone term---gives an approximation ratio of
\[
  O\!\big(\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3}+n^{2/3}\big),
\]
attained at $\theta^\ast=1/(\sqrt{h}\,n^{1/3})$. The bound is round-up dominated when $h\ge n^{2/3}$
and backbone dominated otherwise, the latter reflecting the intrinsic cost of the Directed Steiner
Forest approximation.
